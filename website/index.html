A frontend for Home Assistant

A picture of your home.
Until you reach for it.

Hauser turns a wall tablet or your phone into a calm view of your home. Every room is an illustration that follows the real sun, the weather and the lamps that are on. Touch it and the controls come forward — lights, heating, the calendar, the shopping list. Leave it, and only the picture remains.

Afternoon. The sun does the lighting. Rain outside. It runs down the window, and only the window. Evening. Two lamps are on, and the picture knows which. Night. The last lamp went out, slowly.

This is the living-room screen without its controls. All four states ship with Hauser; your own rooms are drawn from a photograph you take with your phone.

  • It sits on top of Home Assistant

    Hauser does not replace your setup, it gives it a face. Install it as an add-on on Home Assistant OS, or with Docker Compose anywhere else. A guided setup reads your areas and devices, proposes rooms and lets you correct the result. No YAML, no source edits.

  • Made for the wall and for the hand

    On a wall panel or tablet the room picture fills the screen with one narrow control surface beside it. On a phone the rooms are cards under your thumb. Lights, heating, media, the calendar, the shopping list and the household's notes are all there.

  • Yours, and nobody else's

    It runs on your own hardware and talks only to your Home Assistant. No account, no cloud service, no telemetry. Code, design tokens and documentation are open source under the AGPL.

The house is the stage.
The interface is the light on it.

A wall panel is touched for perhaps five per cent of its day. Most home dashboards are designed for those five per cent: tiles, numbers, a grid. Hauser is designed for the other ninety-five. It is the first smart-home interface that people describe not as practical, but as calm and beautiful. Whether that holds is decided in three moments.

01 · The glance

Most of the day, the panel is a picture on the wall. So the picture got the most care.

After a few idle minutes the panel settles into standby: a large clock, the week ahead, the household's post-its, the shopping list, and behind all of it, faintly, the streets of your own neighbourhood, drawn once from OpenStreetMap around the place Home Assistant already knows.

The picture lives with the day. Snow drifts across it in a few flakes at a time; rain is drawn as ink traces in the text colour, quiet enough to stay behind the clock. Deep at night everything but a dimmed red clock goes away. Switch below.

Real captures of the demo. The same minute of the same day, once with the sun up and once with snow falling after dark.

  • Your streets, not a texture. The map is rendered once on the server, as one monochrome file that takes whatever colour the text has. Day and night share it.
  • Everything on it is a door. Touch the shopping list and you are in the list. Touch the week and you are in the calendar. Touch a post-it and you are with that person's reminders. Nothing to unlock first.
  • It knows when to be quiet. Deep at night the screen keeps only a dimmed red clock. The hourly pixel shift that protects the panel is a soft slide, not a jump.

02 · The room

The illustration is the state.

Every room carries an illustration of itself, and the picture is the reading. When the sun sets, the room's evening picture fades in over the length of dusk. When a lamp is on, it is on in the picture. When the last one goes out, the room goes dark.

When it rains outside, the window light turns grey and the rain runs down exactly where the glass is: the picture set knows its windows, so the weather never falls on the sofa. Try the four states.

Sun. Golden window light, the lamps rest. Rain. The set's overcast picture, and rain clipped to the three panes. Evening. The picture shows which lamps are on. Lights off. Faded over the length of dusk, never switched.

The controls, when you want them.

A hand reaches for the panel and finds one narrow control surface beside the picture: rooms, scenes, the lamps, the room's climate. Nothing covers the room. The interface follows the sun through Home Assistant, so nobody in the household ever reaches for a theme setting.

The same panel, the same second. Only the sun moved.

Photograph the room. That is the whole input.

The rooms that ship with Hauser are of one particular home, which is no use to yours. So the room-image wizard draws yours from a phone photograph: choose the crop and the focus point, and it returns the room as a set of pictures for sun, overcast, evening and lights off, with its windows already known, so the rain finds the glass.

Input Evening light, a wide lens, toys on the floor. Nothing staged.

Output The same room. It is the living room in the pictures above.

This is the one place where Hauser talks to a paid third party: the wizard needs your own OpenAI access, and your photo is sent there to be redrawn. That trade is stated before anything is uploaded, every paid step is confirmed by hand, and the wizard is not part of the public demo.

03 · The touch

Light physics.

Every command in Hauser has weight. Light comes on fast and goes out slowly, the way a room does. Nothing jumps; everything fades like dusk. The interface answers before the house does, and when the house disagrees, the control eases back to the truth instead of lying.

Reading lamp

On in a blink Off over the length of dusk

It answers before the house does

Tap a light and it is on, on screen, immediately. The command travels to Home Assistant afterwards. Every intent is tracked and reconciled against the state that comes back.

Undo instead of confirmation

"Everything off" does not ask whether you are sure. It goes out, and for five seconds a small strip offers the way back. Undo is a second command, not a dialog.

A pulse when the house is silent

If no answer arrives within a second, the control pulses softly until it does. If the house says no, the control fades back to the true state. Nothing snaps.

The undo strip after a scene change. The gold bar is the five seconds running out.

One hand, two voices

Paper for the household. Instruments for the house.

Everything a household says to itself is written on paper: post-its in a colour per person, the shopping list grouped by shop, the week as a band, the calendar as a sheet, the energy of the day as notes pinned into a picture. Everything you do to the house is an instrument: gold on a cool ground, tick scales, switches with travel. There is no third voice. No dashboard tiles, no generic grid.

Reminders are post-its per person. The shopping list reads in the order you walk.

The calendar is the paper of the week band, only larger. Your Home Assistant calendars, one sheet.

The energy lives in the picture.

There is no column of numbers. Live load, generation and grid are notes pinned to the sun, the house and the pole in a picture of your home; the day's curve runs along the bottom edge in the same hand. What a house cannot measure is not shown: without a solar meter there is no solar note. No dash stands in for a number, anywhere.

Same language, different hand.

On the phone the rooms are cards with their own pictures, and the cards go dark with the room in the evening. Navigation moves to the thumb as a floating bar. The tokens, the motion and the two voices stay exactly the same.

Weakness with dignity

A wall clock never goes out.

When Home Assistant cannot be reached, the stage stays lit. The controls dim, the connection state is one quiet word in the title bar, and nothing stacks a banner over the room. In standby the clock, the date and the week keep going from the panel itself.

Empty states are designed like the rest: a clock instead of a message, leaving out instead of a dash, a mood instead of a warning. A disconnected home should look like a quiet one, not a broken one.

Technical beta

Built in the open, for one household first.

Hauser installs and runs today. Compatibility with the enormous range of Home Assistant setups is still growing, there is no support SLA, and it is built for trusted deployments on your own network rather than for the public internet. Which version is current, and what changed in it, is in the release notes.

What you get

  • A complete, working interface you can run and read
  • A documented design system, interaction contract and performance budget
  • A guided Home Assistant setup with versioned external configuration
  • AGPL licensed: fork it, run it, modify it; share your changes if you offer it to others

Worth knowing

  • No SLA and no promised response time
  • Device and Home Assistant coverage grows release by release
  • Intended for trusted local networks, not for internet exposure
  • Versions move fast; read the release notes before upgrading

Installations outside this household exist and report back. The first one ran on an Asustor NAS with Docker Compose against Home Assistant Container, with the first light under control ten minutes in. Reports like that shape the next release; the full picture lives in the release notes and the README.

Install

One click on Home Assistant OS. Compose everywhere else.

Both paths end in the same guided setup: it scans Home Assistant, proposes rooms and devices, lets you correct the result and activates a validated configuration in one step. No source edits, no YAML.

Home Assistant OS & Supervised

As a Home Assistant App

  1. Add github.com/ralleur/hauser as an App repository.
  2. Select Hauser, install it, start it.
  3. Open the web UI and run the setup wizard.
Add the repository

A thin wrapper around the same image, with all state in Home Assistant's persistent storage, so App backups cover your household configuration.

Container, Docker hosts, NAS

With Docker Compose

  1. Copy .env.example to .env.
  2. Run docker compose up -d.
  3. Open port 4173 and run the setup wizard.
Read the install guide

Home Assistant Container has no App system, so this is the path there. Backup, restore, update and rollback are documented end to end.
