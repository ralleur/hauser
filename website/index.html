01 / A familiar place

Your rooms.
Instantly familiar.

The sofa by the window. The table where everything happens. Hauser puts the rooms you know at the centre of the screen, with their lights and scenes close by. Start with the included illustrations, upload a picture, or make a set from a photo of your own room.

Optional image generation sends your photo to your chosen provider: OpenAI or Cloudflare. It needs your own access; OpenAI plans or usage charges apply, and Cloudflare’s free allowance has limits. Uploading your own picture needs neither.

02 / The picture tells you

Same room.
A different moment.

By day, a sunlit room. In the evening, a warm glow. After dark, when the last light goes out, the picture settles into darkness. Hauser moves between prepared room images with the time of day and the room’s lights. You can see the change before reading a label.

04 / A personal project

Made for a home.
Shared with yours.

Free. No subscription.

Hauser began on its maker’s wall panel, for the people living in that home. The room you’ve seen here is that living room: sofa, plants, toys and all.

The idea is simple: the person switching on a light shouldn’t have to understand the system behind it. The code is open source, so you can see how it works and make it your own.

Everyday control needs no Hauser account or subscription. Home Assistant runs on your hardware; the iOS app can use Apple Home directly. Optional image generation and other integrations have their own external services.

Still in public beta. Compatibility is growing, and this is a personal project without guaranteed support. The iOS app is a TestFlight beta with time-limited builds. Read what’s changed.

What should I check before installing?

Hauser is beta software. Check the requirements and release notes for your setup. Keep the server on a trusted network; do not expose its port to the internet. See the remote-access guide for access away from home.

What happens if my home is unreachable?

The web panel keeps the room picture and dims its controls when Home Assistant is disconnected. Standby’s clock and date keep running on the panel. Device control needs the connection back. Connection help.

How does the rest of Hauser work?

The feature guide covers the calendar, shopping, media and more, with their integration requirements. For the thinking behind the interface, read the design system and interaction contract. The roadmap distinguishes what works today, what is experimental and what is planned.
